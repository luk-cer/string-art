3D print: line art on a plate

The drawing stands up on a thin plate. Hung in a window or over a light, the plate glows and the lines show dark; in normal light it's a relief. It's always one piece, whatever the style.

1. Drawing

2. Size and line width

Line width: what the printer lays down (0.4 nozzle: 0.6–0.8 mm prints cleanly). The drawing's line spacing must be designed for it, so render it again with pen = line width at the printed size.

3. Plate or free-standing

Free-standing: the white base layers hold the lines plus the shortest bridges that tie every separate piece together and to the rim; the dark layers after the swap hold only the lines, so on a white wall the bridges all but vanish. The web is a sparse white triangle grid under everything: stiffer, still faint on a white wall.
Use multiples of your layer height (0.2 mm): plate 0.6 = 3 layers. Thin plate = brighter glow; taller lines = darker lines when backlit.

Printing (Creality K1C)

Open print.3mf (or print.stl) in Creality Print / OrcaSlicer and print it flat, in white or natural PLA. For black lines on white: on the layer slider, add a pause / filament change at the first layer above the plate (shown below as "swap at"), swap to black, resume. With a multi-colour unit, load the 3MF as one object with two parts and give each a filament.
Pick a drawing, then press "Make 3D files".
